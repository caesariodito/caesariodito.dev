# Decision Framework

I've been struggling with decision fatigue around which projects to take on and how to prioritize my time. Today I finally sat down and created a structured framework to guide these choices.

## The Problem

As developers, we face a constant stream of decisions:

- Which technologies to learn
- Which projects to contribute to
- How to balance deep work vs. collaboration
- When to refactor vs. when to ship

Without a framework, I've been making these decisions based on a mix of urgency, excitement, and people-pleasing—often leading to commitments I later regret.

## The Framework

I've developed a simple scoring system based on four criteria:

1. **Growth Impact** (1-10): How much will this help me develop valuable skills?
2. **Joy Factor** (1-10): How energizing will working on this be?
3. **Alignment** (1-10): How well does this fit with my [[WorkLifePhilosophy]]?
4. **Opportunity Cost** (-10-0): What am I giving up to do this?

For each decision, I calculate: `Growth + Joy + Alignment + Cost`

Anything scoring below 15 is an automatic no. 15-20 requires careful consideration. Above 20 is usually a yes.

## Application Examples

I applied this to recent decisions:

| Decision              | Growth | Joy | Alignment | Cost | Total | Choice |
| --------------------- | ------ | --- | --------- | ---- | ----- | ------ |
| Weekend hackathon     | 8      | 7   | 4         | -8   | 11    | No     |
| Learning Rust         | 9      | 8   | 7         | -3   | 21    | Yes    |
| Extra project at work | 4      | 3   | 2         | -7   | 2     | No     |

This immediately clarified why I've been feeling drained—I've been saying yes to too many low-scoring opportunities.

## Connection to Other Practices

This framework builds on insights from my [[BurnoutRecovery]] experience, particularly around recognizing the importance of joy and alignment in sustainable productivity.

It also complements my #mindfulness practice from [[MindfulnessMeditation]], as it creates space between stimulus (opportunity arises) and response (decision to commit).

## Growth Moment

The most powerful aspect of this #decision-making framework isn't the scoring itself—it's that it forces me to explicitly consider factors I was previously ignoring.

By applying #systems-thinking to my choices, I'm creating guardrails that naturally guide me toward #productivity that's sustainable and meaningful rather than frantic and depleting.

I plan to refine this framework over time as I learn which factors truly predict satisfaction and impact in my work.
