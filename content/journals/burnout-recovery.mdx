# Burnout Recovery

Six weeks ago, I hit a wall. After three consecutive project crunches and too many late nights, I woke up one morning and couldn't bring myself to open my laptop. Classic burnout symptoms: exhaustion, cynicism, and a feeling of ineffectiveness despite working longer hours.

## The Warning Signs I Missed

Looking back, there were clear signals I ignored:

1. Dreading opening pull requests I'd normally be excited about
2. Difficulty focusing during meetings
3. Coding solutions I knew were suboptimal just to "get it done"
4. Skipping my [[MindfulnessMeditation]] sessions because I "didn't have time"
5. Increasing reliance on caffeine

## The Recovery Plan

After acknowledging the burnout, I created a structured recovery approach:

### 1. Immediate Relief

- Took a full week off with no code, no Slack
- Spent time in nature and with non-tech hobbies
- Resumed my #meditation practice

### 2. Root Cause Analysis

- Reflected on my [[WorkLifePhilosophy]] and where I'd strayed from it
- Identified that I hadn't been setting proper #boundaries with stakeholders
- Recognized a pattern of saying yes to prevent disappointing others

### 3. Sustainable Return

- Came back with explicit working hours (9-5 only)
- Scheduled breaks between focus sessions
- Applied techniques from [[RefactoringMeditation]] to my work approach

## What's Working

The most effective changes have been:

- **Time boxing**: I now work in 90-minute focused sessions with 15-minute breaks
- **No-meeting Wednesdays**: I've blocked my calendar for deep work
- **Weekly review**: I assess my energy levels and adjust the next week accordingly
- **#work-life-balance guardrails**: My computer automatically locks at 6pm

## Growth Moment

The biggest realization has been that #burnout isn't just about working too much—it's about working in a way that's disconnected from meaning and proper boundaries.

I'm now seeing that sustainable productivity isn't about heroic effort; it's about consistent, meaningful contribution that respects my human limitations. As I continue implementing my [[DecisionFramework]], I'm making sure that "Will this lead to burnout?" is a key question in evaluating opportunities.

Recovery isn't linear, but I'm grateful for the wake-up call that forced me to rebuild my relationship with work in a healthier way.
