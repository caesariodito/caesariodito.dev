# The Art of Code Review

Today I reviewed a substantial PR from one of our newer team members. It was a feature we'd been planning for weeks, and I was excited to see it come to life. However, I noticed several issues with the implementation that needed addressing.

## The Reflection

In the past, I might have just listed all the problems and requested changes. But I've been thinking a lot about how #communication in code reviews shapes our #team-culture. So I tried a different approach:

1. Started with genuine appreciation for the work done
2. Asked questions instead of making assertions
3. Offered suggestions as alternatives rather than corrections

For example, instead of:

> This won't scale well with large datasets. Use memoization here.

I wrote:

> I really like how clean this implementation is! For larger datasets, have you considered how it might perform? [[Memoization]] could be a nice optimization here if you think it's needed.

## The Result

The response was noticeably different from previous reviews. Instead of a defensive explanation, I got thoughtful consideration and even some counter-suggestions that improved on my initial idea. We ended up with a hybrid approach that was better than either of our original thoughts.

This connects to my earlier thoughts on [[TeamCommunication]] and reinforces the idea that #code-review is as much about people as it is about code.

## Growth Moment

I'm realizing that technical leadership isn't just about having the right answers—it's about creating an environment where everyone feels empowered to contribute their best thinking. The "right" technical solution emerges from collaborative dialogue, not from top-down directives.

Next time, I want to experiment with asking even more open-ended questions to see what creative solutions might emerge that I wouldn't have thought of on my own.
